# Evidence matrix and report specification

## Evidence matrix

Maintain a working matrix with these fields:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Title | Complete paper title |
| URL | CNKI detail-page URL |
| Bibliography | Author, year, source, resource type, DOI when available |
| Quality evidence | Source tier, impact/citation evidence, quality confidence, and cautions |
| Evidence level | A, B, or C |
| Research role | Review, foundational, recent primary, method, contrary, or contextual |
| Supported point | Narrow paraphrase actually supported by the available evidence |
| Location | Checked section, result, conclusion, or abstract basis |
| Limitations | Scope, method, sample, access, metadata, or conflict limitations |
| Theme | Report theme or subquestion served |

Do not expose the whole internal matrix when it would overwhelm the user. Include a compact table of the sources that materially support the report.

## Claim rules

1. Support substantive factual claims with A or B evidence.
2. Use B evidence only within the scope of the abstract; do not invent methods, numbers, or limitations absent from it.
3. Never use C evidence as substantive support.
4. Mark synthesis with phrases such as “综合这些研究可推断” when the conclusion is the agent's inference rather than a paper's explicit statement.
5. Describe disagreement before explaining possible causes such as population, period, method, definitions, or jurisdiction.
6. Prefer paraphrase. Quote only short text that is necessary and verified.
7. Link the complete paper title to its CNKI detail page at first meaningful citation.

## Default report structure

### Title

Use a precise topic and scope.

### Executive summary

Answer the central question in 3–6 concise findings. Identify confidence and the most important unresolved issue.

### 1. Question and scope

State the research question, subquestions, disciplines, time range, exclusions, and any assumptions.

### 2. Search and evidence method

Summarize query families, search modes, date bounds, inclusion/exclusion logic, metadata screening, full-text access, evidence levels, and stopping condition. Do not describe an unrecorded process.

### 3. Field development

Explain chronology, foundational concepts, major shifts, and representative sources where the evidence supports this history.

### 4. Major themes

Organize by mechanisms, theories, methods, populations, jurisdictions, or applications—not one paragraph per paper. Compare sources within each theme.

### 5. Disputes and conflicting evidence

Present competing positions, their evidence, and plausible reasons for disagreement. Preserve minority findings when credible.

### 6. Methods and evidence quality

Assess dominant designs, datasets, samples, measurements, source coverage, quality-confidence limits, and transferability.

### 7. Gaps and future directions

Derive gaps from the reviewed evidence. Distinguish an observed gap from a speculative research opportunity.

### 8. Conclusion

Directly answer the main question. Calibrate confidence and avoid stronger certainty than the evidence allows.

### Evidence table

Include the most consequential sources, their roles, evidence levels, and limitations.

### Linked references

List complete titles with CNKI detail links and concise bibliographic information. Do not include unused discovery-only results in the main bibliography.

### Search appendix

List query families, years, major filters, number of useful candidates, full-text availability, contrary-evidence search, and whether work stopped because of saturation, budget, or access.

## Quality checks before delivery

- Every major finding traces to at least one A or B source.
- No paper is represented more strongly than its available evidence permits.
- The report includes contrary or limiting evidence when it exists.
- Themes synthesize multiple papers instead of concatenating summaries.
- The title links point to detail pages, not search-result pages.
- Search limitations and access gaps are explicit.
- “Systematic review” is used only when the process meets that claim.
